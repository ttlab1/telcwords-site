How to Learn TELC Vocabulary: A Study Plan with Tips

Vocabulary is the part of exam preparation you can improve fastest. Grammar takes time to sink in, but a new word can be yours in a few days if you meet it often enough. This guide gives you a plan that works for any telc level from A1 to C1 and takes about fifteen minutes a day.

It is built around six steps, with real numbers from the TELC Words lists: how long each list takes at different speeds, a sample week, how to use the different game modes, and how to learn nouns, verbs and phrases so that you can actually use them.

The plan in short

  • Pick your level and learn 10–20 new words a day, plus review.
  • Learn every noun with its article and plural, every verb with a short sentence.
  • Practise in short daily sessions and always play the bonus round with your mistakes.
  • From B1, learn verbs with prepositions as one unit, with the case.
  • Use new words in your own sentences, out loud.

So if you learn 20 new words a day, the B1 list takes about 105 days, or roughly three and a half months. The A1 list takes about 55 days at the same speed. Two things make the real time shorter or longer:

  • The lists overlap. 1,000 of the 2,098 B1 entries also appear in our A1 or A2 list. If you already know A2 well, the B1 list has fewer truly new words for you.
  • Review takes time too. A new word is not learned after one look. Plan to spend about as much time on review as on new words. If that feels like too much, choose 10 new words a day instead of 20. A slower plan you keep is better than a fast plan you stop after a week.

The six steps

1. Pick the right level

Choose the level of your exam: A1, A2, B1, B2 or C1. Then test yourself: play two or three blocks at that level. If you know almost every word and never lose a heart, your base is good and you can focus on the words you miss. If you keep losing hearts in the first blocks, step down one level for a week or two and rebuild the base. Words from the lower lists come back again and again at higher levels, so the time is not lost.

Scroll through the full list of your level once, too. It shows you which topics you need and where you have gaps.

2. Learn nouns with their article and plural

Gender is part of the word. Do not learn "Wohnung", learn die Wohnung (apartment). The article decides the adjective endings and the pronoun, so a wrong gender causes several mistakes in one sentence. Our guide to der, die, das shows which noun endings are reliable (every noun ending in -ung in our lists is feminine) and which exceptions to learn by heart.

Add the plural as soon as you can, because it is often not predictable: die Wohnung → die Wohnungen, der Termin → die Termine, das Zimmer → die Zimmer. Say the three forms together: "der Termin, die Termine". It takes one second longer and saves you from guessing later.

In the game, nouns are colour-coded by article: der blue, die pink, das green. After a while, the colour comes back to you together with the word.

3. Practise in short daily sessions

A block of ten words takes about two minutes. Do a few blocks a day. Regular repetition moves words into long-term memory much better than a single long session, because your brain keeps what it needs again and again. A simple rhythm is to meet a new word on the day you learn it, again the next day, again a few days later and again about a week later. Each time it comes back a little faster.

Link the session to something you already do every day: the bus ride, the coffee after lunch, the ten minutes before you sleep. A fixed time is easier to keep than a good intention.

4. Review your mistakes

Words you answer wrongly return in the bonus round. Those are the words worth your time, so do not skip it. Many learners spend most of their time on words they already know, because it feels good. The progress is in the mistakes.

Keep a short list of words you miss more than once, on paper or in your phone. Look at it every day for one minute. When you get a word right three days in a row, cross it out.

5. Learn verbs and phrases, not only words

Verbs: learn each verb with a short sentence, not alone. Many common verbs are separable: anrufen (to call) becomes "Ich rufe dich an", and the Perfekt is "angerufen". Our list of separable verbs shows all of them from the A1, A2 and B1 lists, with the rules for word order. Reflexive verbs come with sich, for example sich beeilen (to hurry): learn the sich as part of the verb.

Phrases from B1 upward: texts at B1 and above use many verbs with a fixed preposition. Learn the verb, the preposition and the case as one unit. Our lists show many of them in this form:

  • abhängen von (to depend on): Das hängt vom Wetter ab.
  • sich um eine Stelle bewerben (to apply for a job): Ich bewerbe mich um eine Stelle als Koch.
  • sich wenden an (to turn to / contact) and Bezug nehmen auf (to refer to)
  • verzichten auf (+ Akk.) (to do without / give up)
  • sich auseinandersetzen mit (+ Dat.) (to engage / grapple with)
  • Stellung nehmen zu (+ Dat.) (to comment on)

The note "(+ Akk.)" or "(+ Dat.)" tells you the case after the preposition. Learn it together with the phrase, because it changes the article: "auf den Urlaub", but "mit dem Thema".

6. Use the words

Write one sentence for every few new words and say it aloud. Active use fixes a word better than recognition alone. Recognising a word in the game is the first step. Being able to say it without help is the second, and that is what you need when you speak or write.

Make the sentences about your own life: your job, your flat, your family, your plans. They are easier to remember, and they are the kind of sentences you will need anyway. Once a week, read your sentences again and correct them if you can.

How to use the game modes

The TELC Words game has different ways to practise the same lists:

  • Meanings: you see a German word and pick the English meaning from six options before the 10-second timer runs out. You have three hearts, and fast correct answers build streaks up to ×4. Use it for new words and for speed.
  • Articles: you see a noun and tap der, die or das. Use it after you know the meaning of the nouns, to fix the gender. Example: B1 articles.
  • B1 topics: when you choose B1, you can play all B1 words or one of 10 topics (636 words in total). Use topics before a speaking or writing practice on that theme.

A sample weekly routine

This is one example for about 15 minutes a day with 15 new words on weekdays. Change it to fit your life; the important part is that review comes every day.

DayWhat to do
Mon–Fri5 minutes: review yesterday's words. 7 minutes: 15 new words in Meanings mode, with the bonus round. 3 minutes: write two or three sentences with new words and say them aloud.
SaturdayNo new words. Play the Articles mode for your level, then go through your list of difficult words.
SundayLight day: one or two blocks for fun, or a B1 topic you will need soon. Read your sentences from the week.

That is 75 new words a week, about 300 a month. With this plan the A2 list takes about 14 weeks.

Common mistakes

  • Learning nouns without the article. Fixing the gender later is much harder than learning it at the start.
  • Too many new words, too little review. Fifty new words on Monday and none for the rest of the week does not work.
  • Only reading the list. Reading feels easy, but testing yourself is what makes words stick.
  • Skipping the bonus round. It contains exactly the words you do not know yet.
  • Learning single verbs without their pattern. "bewerben" alone is not enough; learn "sich um eine Stelle bewerben".
  • Cramming in the last week. Short, regular sessions over months beat long sessions just before the exam.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I study vocabulary each day?

Ten to twenty focused minutes a day usually beats a long weekly session, because frequent review keeps words in long-term memory. In TELC Words, a block of ten words takes about two minutes, so five to eight blocks fit into a short daily session.

How many new words should I learn per day?

Ten to twenty new words a day is a realistic target for most learners, plus review of older words. At 20 new words a day, our A1 list (1,099 words) takes about 55 days and our B1 list (2,098 words) about 105 days. These numbers are about the TELC Words lists, not an official requirement.

Should I learn words by level or by topic?

Start with your exam level, then group words by topic when reviewing. Topics give context, which helps recall. For B1, the game has a topic mode with 10 themes such as work, housing and health.

Do I need to learn the article and plural of every noun?

Yes, learn every noun with its article from the start, because the gender changes the article, adjective endings and pronouns. Add the plural as soon as you can. Learning "der Termin, die Termine" takes only a second longer than "Termin" alone.

How do I learn verbs with prepositions?

Learn the verb, the preposition and the case as one unit, for example "verzichten auf (+ Akk.)", and write one short sentence with it. Our C1 list shows many entries in this form.
