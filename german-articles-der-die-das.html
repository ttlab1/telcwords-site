German Articles: How to Learn der, die, das

Every German noun has a gender: masculine (der), feminine (die) or neuter (das). The gender is not always predictable, and it changes the article, adjective endings and pronouns. For telc exams from A1 to C1, knowing the gender of a noun is part of knowing the word.

The most useful habit: learn the article with the noun

Never memorise "Tisch". Memorise "der Tisch". In TELC Words the article is colour-coded (blue for der, pink for die, green for das), so your memory links the colour and the word.

Patterns that help

Usually feminine (die)

  • -ung: die Wohnung, die Zeitung
  • -heit / -keit: die Gesundheit, die Möglichkeit
  • -schaft: die Freundschaft
  • -ion: die Information

Usually masculine (der)

  • days, months and seasons: der Montag, der Mai, der Winter
  • -er for people: der Lehrer, der Fahrer
  • -ismus: der Tourismus

Usually neuter (das)

  • -chen and -lein (diminutives): das Mädchen
  • -ment: das Dokument
  • nouns made from verbs: das Essen, das Lernen

These are tendencies, not laws. There are exceptions, so keep checking new nouns.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a rule for der, die, das?

There are useful patterns, such as most nouns ending in -ung, -heit, -keit and -schaft being feminine, but many nouns have no rule. The safest method is to learn every noun with its article.

Do articles matter in the TELC exam?

Yes. Gender affects articles, adjective endings and pronouns, so errors cost points in writing, speaking and the language-elements part.
